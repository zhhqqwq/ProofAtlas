# ProofAtlas Integrated Skill v0.2 — Pipeline Adversarial Test Report

Scope: orchestration-level tests over the nine existing Construction Archaeology / CPG proof cases.

Purpose: verify that the Skill chooses the correct internal depth and produces a learner-facing projection without violating upstream protocol boundaries.

## Evaluation dimensions

For each proof, test:
- mode/depth routing;
- hotspot selection;
- M3 use only where justified;
- CA Unit/Episode selectivity;
- ConstructionPolicy handling;
- constant/constraint provenance;
- alternative Route preservation;
- CPG scope/identity integrity;
- presentation-compression explanation;
- learner-facing output discipline.

## Case 1 — x^2 continuity

Expected:
- localization hotspot;
- M3/CA on localization;
- constraint merge;
- constant provenance;
- presentation compression.

Must explain why |x+a| is the obstacle, why a local restriction is useful, why 1 is convenient rather than necessary, and why the final min merges two requirements.

Must not turn factorization algebra into a separate Construction Unit.

Result: **PASS**

## Case 2 — 1/x continuity

Expected:
- denominator-safety hotspot;
- parameter family theta in (0,1);
- specialization theta=1/2;
- merge of denominator and epsilon requirements.

Repeated 1/2 occurrences should share one specialization provenance rather than generating multiple magic-number stories.

Result: **PASS**

## Case 3 — differentiable implies continuous

Expected:
- shallow map;
- one representation hotspot;
- little or no full Constraint Ledger;
- no Constant Archaeology.

The key transformation aligns the target difference with the derivative interface.

Result: **PASS**

This is the anti-overanalysis control case.

## Case 4 — uniform limit preserves continuity

Expected:
- bridge / approximant mechanism;
- error budget;
- N before delta dependency;
- stable identity for f_N;
- learner explanation emphasizes transfer of control through an approximant.

M3 targets the bridge/approximant choice, not every epsilon estimate.

Result: **PASS**

## Case 5 — Heine–Cantor

Expected:
- contradiction route;
- bad-pair construction;
- existential epsilon_0 distinguished from chosen 1/n;
- compactness theorem role;
- branch/subproof scope isolation.

Learner-facing explanation should describe the structural contradiction without exposing raw scope records.

Result: **PASS**

## Case 6 — limsup approximating subsequence

Expected:
- recursive ConstructionPolicy;
- child Episode allowed by Episode Substance Gate;
- state/invariant/progress explicit internally;
- generated subsequence distinct from policy.

The teaching view should explain the recurring selection rule, not merely show the finished subsequence.

Result: **PASS**

## Case 7 — positive continuous function on compact set

Expected:
- preserve reference contradiction route;
- preserve EVT alternative if independently available;
- never blend them into one hybrid discovery story.

In compare mode, compare mechanisms and theorem dependence without forcing a universal winner.

Result: **PASS**

## Case 8 — interval self-map fixed point

Expected:
- residual construction g=f-id;
- theorem preparation separated from IVT invocation;
- no Constant Archaeology on endpoints 0 and 1, which are problem data;
- canonical-proof discovery claims calibrated to M3-provisional unless memorization resistance is tested.

Result: **PASS**

## Case 9 — uniqueness of sequence limits

Expected:
- bridge through a_n;
- two-error budget;
- independent threshold events N_1 || N_2;
- max merge;
- presentation order not confused with causal independence.

Result: **PASS**

## Cross-case result

**9/9 pipeline cases PASS at the protocol-contract level.**

The integration did not expose a need for a new core ontology or proof-analysis layer.

The product-level requirement is now:

selective depth
+ fidelity isolation
+ progressive disclosure.
